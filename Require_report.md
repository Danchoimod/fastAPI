A PPP (Progress, Plans, Problems) report is a concise, weekly status update structured into three sections to align teams and track goals. It highlights key achievements, upcoming priorities, and roadblocks to foster transparency and efficiency. Keep it to one page, focusing on high-impact items rather than every small task.


PPP Report Structure
Progress (What you did): List 3-5 completed achievements from the reporting period. Focus on results and impact rather than just tasks. Use data to show progress, such as percentage completion or specific metrics.
Plans (What you will do): List 3-5 main goals or priorities for the next period. These should be actionable and align with overall objectives.
Problems (What is blocking you): Identify obstacles or bottlenecks that are preventing progress and require help or resources. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]

Best Practices
Be Concise: Keep it brief. Use bullet points for readability.
Weekly Frequency: Best sent weekly, perhaps via email, Slack, or shared documents like Google Docs or Excel.
Be Honest: Focus on transparency, particularly in the "Problems" section, to allow for faster resolution.
Action-Oriented: "Plans" for one week often become "Progress" the next. [1, 2, 3]

Example Format
Progress
Completed Q2 marketing strategy draft (100%).
Onboarded new content creator.
Resolved API integration issue with developers.
Plans

Launch email campaign for new product.

Finalize budget proposal for Q3.

Conduct competitor analysis for Q4.
Problems
Waiting on legal approval for marketing materials (Risk of 2-day delay).
Need budget approval for new software tools.
